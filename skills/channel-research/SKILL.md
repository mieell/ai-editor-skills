# Channel research

1. Verify the handle or channel ID against YouTube. Inventory the relevant public tabs and use a consistent sample. Check direct video pages when you need exact counts, dates, or durations. Record the capture date, time zone used for publication dates, and any discrepancy between the channel header and the verifiable catalog.
2. Sample the hook, middle, and payoff of representative uploads. Record title framing, first visual, pace, caption treatment, visual style, and recurring formats. Use transcripts or audio when available. Label frame-only observations accordingly.
3. Compare like with like. Use medians or explicit early/later cohorts where informative. Distinguish cumulative public metrics from audience retention or traffic data, which require channel analytics. Report patterns as observations when upload date, format, or topic overlap.
4. Cite the channel and individual uploads near the claims they support. End with the editorial choices the evidence supports, such as the opening image, a usable caption treatment, or a repeatable story structure.
